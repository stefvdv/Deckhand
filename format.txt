[full-rules]
Admiral is a singleton format for the cards of Standard's whole history, led by a legendary Admiral and built on a shared Landing Zone that replaces lands in decks entirely. These are the full rules.
== Your Admiral
Choose a legendary card with power and toughness printed on its front side as the Admiral of your deck. That means legendary creatures — and other legendary cards with printed power and toughness, such as Vehicles — while planeswalkers and battles don't qualify.
Being an Admiral is not a characteristic [CR 109.3]; it is a property tied directly to the physical card. "Admiral-ness" cannot be copied or overwritten by continuous effects. The card remains your Admiral through any status changes, and is still your Admiral even while another player controls it.
== Deckbuilding
An Admiral deck contains exactly 60 cards, including its Admiral. Every card must have been legal in Standard — or its predecessor, Type 2 — at some point in its history, and every card must have a nonland front side (modal double-faced cards with a land front are not eligible).
Singleton: no two cards in the deck may share an English name. Some cards (e.g. Relentless Rats) carry rules text that overrides this restriction.
A card's color identity is its color plus the colors of any mana symbols in its rules text. Color identity is established before the game begins and cannot be changed by game effects. No card in the deck may have a color in its identity that isn't in the color identity of the deck's Admiral.
To keep play immersed in the world of Magic itself, an Admiral deck contains only cards from within Magic: The Gathering's own multiverse — Universes Beyond cards are not part of the format.
> Why the hard line? Admiral is built to feel like Magic: one connected multiverse with a consistent story, style and identity. Crossover sets are wonderful products, but they are written for other worlds first — and a format is at its best when its card pool tells one coherent story.
**Rule Zero.** Like every casual Magic format, Admiral games begin with the unwritten rule that outranks all written ones: your playgroup decides together what your games look like. If your table wants to welcome Universes Beyond cards, that is a perfectly legitimate use of Rule Zero — agree on it before the game begins. The app supports exactly this: the "Include all Standard-legal sets" switch in Extras opens the full pool and extends the banned list to cover it.
== The Landing Zone
Decks contain no lands. Instead, the game begins with a shared zone called the Landing Zone, stocked with an unlimited supply of basic lands of every type, including Wastes. Snow versions are available too: whenever you take a basic from the Landing Zone, you choose whether it is the snow version.
The **corresponding basic** for a card follows its color identity — white corresponds to Plains, blue to Island, black to Swamp, red to Mountain, green to Forest, and a colorless identity to Wastes. For a multicolored card, you choose one of its colors when you take the basic.
**Playing a land.** Whenever you could normally play a land from a zone (hand, the top of your library, and so on), you instead exile that card face up from that zone and put a corresponding basic onto the battlefield from the Landing Zone. This is a special action: it doesn't use the stack, can't be responded to, counts as your land play, and exiling the card this way doesn't trigger any abilities or effects.
Effects that let you play lands from another zone — the graveyard, exile, or the top of your library — work exactly the same way, even though no actual land cards are there: you exile a card from that zone and take the corresponding basic. A Perennial Behemoth lets you exile a card from your graveyard for a basic; a Courser of Kruphix, from the top of your library. Effects that grant additional land plays grant additional exchanges.
> Example — with Courser of Kruphix and Perennial Behemoth in play, during your main phase with an empty stack, you may exile a card from your hand, your graveyard, or the top of your library to put a corresponding basic onto the battlefield.
**Effects that move lands.** If a spell or ability instructs you to put one or more lands from any zone other than the battlefield into another zone, you instead exile that many cards face up from the source zone — up to a maximum of five per instruction — and put corresponding basics into the destination zone as part of resolving it, tapped or untapped as the effect specifies. Exiling cards this way doesn't trigger any abilities or effects. If the effect asks for lands of specific types, the cards you exile must have the matching colors in their identity. Lands already on the battlefield change zones normally.
> Examples — Cultivate: exile up to two cards from your library; a corresponding basic enters the battlefield tapped and another goes to your hand. Splendid Reclamation: exile up to five cards from your graveyard and put that many corresponding basics onto the battlefield. Borborygmos Enraged: exile a card from your hand, put a corresponding basic into your hand, then discard it to the ability.
**Reveals.** When an effect reveals cards and refers to lands among them, each revealed nonland card may be treated as a land of its corresponding types — but only for that effect's land-related instructions. Everything else about the card is unchanged.
> Examples — Satyr Wayfinder: reveal the top four cards; you may choose one as your "land", exiling it to put a corresponding basic into your hand, and the rest go to your graveyard. Destroy the Evidence: the destroyed land's controller reveals cards until revealing a "land" — the first card revealed qualifies — and it goes to the graveyard with the rest.
**After the Zone.** A basic taken from the Landing Zone is owned by the player who put it onto the battlefield. From then on it is a normal card: if it's destroyed it goes to that player's graveyard, if bounced to their hand, and so on — it never returns to the Landing Zone.
== Casting your Admiral
Admirals begin the game in the command zone. While your Admiral is in the command zone, you may cast it, subject to the normal timing restrictions for its card types. You must pay an additional {2} for each time it was previously cast from the command zone.
If your Admiral is in a graveyard or in exile and was put there since the last time state-based actions were checked, you may put it into the command zone. If your Admiral would be put into your hand or library from anywhere, you may put it into the command zone instead; this replacement effect may apply more than once to the same event.
== Winning & Losing
Mulligans work as in Commander: the London mulligan, with the first mulligan free in multiplayer games.
Players begin the game with 30 life. All normal ways to win and lose apply — and in addition, a player who has been dealt 18 or more combat damage by a particular Admiral over the course of the game loses the game.
